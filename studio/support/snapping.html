Snapping & drawing aids

Snaps are what make a CAD program feel good or feel awful, so they get their own row on the toolbar and their own function keys. The rule the Studio holds itself to: a snap mark appears if and only if clicking right now would commit that snap.

What a snap looks like

When a snap is live you get a hollow blue ring at the exact point the click will take, with a bright halo so it reads over any linework, and the object it is snapping to lights up in pale blue. The status readout names the snap in words — “Endpoint snap”, “Dimension midpoint”.

Weaker snaps — Near, ortho, alignment — draw the same ring more faintly, so a strong snap a few pixels away visibly wins. If you want the kind named right beside the cursor, turn on Snap kind labels in Display & Defaults.

A soft grey circle that follows your cursor is not a snap. That is just the cursor ghost telling you where the point tool currently is.

The pills

OnF3 — the master. Off means every snap and magnet stops, and objects land exactly where you click.
EndF7 — endpoints.
CenF6 — centres of couplings, fittings, valves, heads, hangers and drafting shapes.
MidShift+F5 — pipe and segment midpoints, and the middle of a drafted dimension.
PipeF4 — pipe centreline. Also the master gate for Perp, Near and Int.
PerpF8 or F9 — perpendicular.
NearQuarter-inch stations along an object. No function key — see the note below.
IntF10 — crossings, both plan crossings and true 3D ones.
TileCeiling tile centres from an imported ceiling grid. The sprinkler tool's best friend.
Lt·MidOn ceilings with no grid, locks to the midpoint between two nearby light fixtures.
AlignMagnetic smart guides — pulls into line with existing head rows, columns and pipe ends, and draws the dashed guide.
CtrDead centre of the live dimension under a head you are placing — the point where both readouts show the same number.
Tile ✕Shows a mark at every spot the tile magnet can land a head. A view aid; never plots.
GridThe drawing grid, at the Grid size on the second toolbar row.
Near has no function key right now. It used to be F12, and F12 now opens the bug reporter from anywhere — which wins. Toggle Near from its pill.

Tab cycles the candidates

When several snaps are competing under your cursor, press Tab to step through them. The status bar names each one as you land on it; click to take it. Move the mouse and the cycle ends.

Mid-draw you can also force one snap for the next pick only: E endpoint, C centre, M midpoint, N near. Esc cancels the one-shot.

Ortho, elevation lock, and rounding

  • Ortho (Alt+F2) locks to the axis. Hold Shift to flip it temporarily — the pill shows the state you are actually getting. Ortho is a hard axis lock: a snap decides where along the axis you land, never whether you leave it. 45° runs are included.
  • Elev Lock (Shift+F2, or the tick on the second toolbar row) holds the elevation in the Elev box while you draw, even when you snap to something at a different height.
  • Rounding snap (Shift+F3) rounds picks to a clean increment.

Ortho is a mode, not a snap, so it keeps working with F3 off — and it still shows its mark, so the plan always says why the point moved.

In progress: ortho behaves identically everywhere except two places — dragging a drafting shape's vertex grip, and dragging a building element's grip. Those two do not have an ortho base point yet. Everything else — drawing, moving heads, pipe bodies, coverage cell edges — uses the same hard lock.

The magnets

Beyond the classic object snaps, the Studio pulls toward things a sprinkler designer aims at anyway:

  • Ceiling tile centres, including the two quarter points of a 2×4.
  • The centreline of a room, and the centre point between two walls, two heads, or a wall and a head.
  • Column centres.
  • Existing head rows and columns, from about a foot out, with a dashed guide line to show what you caught.
  • Parallel alignment with existing linework while you are placing the end of a new line.

All of them die with F3.

Rhythm assist — the program pedalling with you

Place three heads in a rhythm — the same spacing, the same position in each bay, the same station along a pipe — and a faint ghost of the next one appears.

  • Tab, or a click on the ghost, places it. The next prediction appears immediately, so Tab-Tab-Tab walks the row.
  • Hold Tab to sweep — placements flow with a running count in the readout and stop at the first one that would break a rule or leave the pattern. The whole sweep is one undo.
  • . repeats your last placement; hold it to sweep the same way.
  • Ignoring a ghost costs nothing. Keep working and it goes away; ignore it often enough and the assist backs off by itself.

A ghost only appears for a placement that would pass the same rules as a manual click — it will not offer you a head inside an obstruction.

When snapping fights you

  1. F3. If the problem disappears, a snap was the cause.
  2. Turn off the specific pill. Near is the usual culprit — it can slide a pick along a line you did not mean to touch.
  3. Reduce Snap tol on the second toolbar row so the aperture is tighter.
  4. Hide or lock the layer you keep catching. Imported linework under your cursor is the most common source of a wrong pick.
  5. Use Tab to take the one you actually want rather than fighting the ranking.

If a snap mark appears somewhere the click refuses to go, that is a bug by definition — press F12 and circle it.
